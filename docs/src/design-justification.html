1Product, users and design principles

Ortak Hesap helps students who share a flat, a dorm room or a holiday keep track of who paid for what and who owes whom. Our users are 18–26 years old, use the phone one-handed (in a shop, a café, on the bus), often in the evening, and are not accountants: they want to log a purchase in a few seconds and see one clear answer, “how much do I owe or get back?”. Money between friends is also socially sensitive, so the interface must feel calm and fair rather than alarming. This led to three principles:

Speed to addAmount first, sensible defaults (paid by me, split equally), one primary action per screen.
Clarity of moneyOne headline balance per group, who-owes-whom in plain words, never colour alone.
Calm, fair toneFriendly teal and warm terracotta instead of banking blue and alarm red.

2Colour palette

We built a Material 3 scheme from the seed colour #0B6B63 (deep teal) and tuned two roles by hand. Teal signals trust and money but stays distinct from the blue of most banking apps, and reads as “growth” for money you are owed. Debts use a warm terracotta (tertiary role) instead of red: red is reserved for errors and destructive actions, so a normal debt does not feel like a warning.

Colour is never the only signal. Teal and terracotta have almost the same luminance, so a colour-blind user (or a screenshot in greyscale) could confuse them. Every amount therefore also carries a sign (+ / −), an arrow icon and a text label (“owes you” / “you owe”).

3Typography

Plus Jakarta Sans (SemiBold/Bold) is used for display, headline and title styles. Its geometric, slightly rounded letterforms feel friendly and modern, which suits a social-money app and gives the group names and balances character. Inter is used for body, labels and every amount: it is designed for small screens, stays legible at 12 sp and offers tabular figures, so digits line up in columns of amounts. Both families fully support Turkish characters (ç ğ ı İ ö ş ü) and the lira sign, which matters for our users.

We follow the Material 3 type scale with sizes in sp, so text grows with the user’s system font setting (the layouts are designed to survive 200 %). The smallest size is 12 sp, used only for captions.

4Layout and navigation

  • Thumb-first, compact layout. We design for a 360×800 dp phone first. A Material 3 navigation bar with three destinations (Groups, Activity, Profile) sits at the bottom, and the extended FAB “Add expense” is placed bottom-right, inside the natural thumb zone. The most frequent action is therefore one tap away from any group screen.
  • Progressive disclosure. The Add-expense form opens with only amount, title and payer; the split defaults to “equally” and the custom-split sheet appears only on request.
  • Consistent grid and shapes. 8 dp grid, 16 dp margins, 48×48 dp minimum touch targets, M3 corner radii (12 dp cards and fields, 28 dp FAB and sheets), tonal surfaces instead of heavy shadows.
  • Plain-language balances. The Balances screen lists the minimal set of payments (“Ayşe pays Mert ₺120,00”), each with one “Settle” button and a confirmation dialog, because paying back is the goal of the app.
  • Helpful states and local formatting. Empty states give one next step, errors appear inline with an icon and text, finished groups show “All settled”. Amounts follow Turkish conventions (₺1.250,50) and texts stay short for TR/EN.

6How the design supports the intended users

Students open the app in short bursts, so the design favours recognition over reading: one large balance, Material 3 components they already know from Android, and a gentle colour scheme that does not make a debt feel like an accusation. In the prototype, motion is used sparingly and with purpose: the FAB expands into the Add-expense sheet (container transform) so users see where the form comes from, a new expense slides into the list, and the balance counts to its new value after a settlement, confirming that the action worked.
